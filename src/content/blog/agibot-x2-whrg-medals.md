**AgiBot X2** won the 100-meter obstacle race at the second World Humanoid Robot Games in its mass-produced configuration, with no hardware changes for the meet, according to Gizmochina and Humanoids Daily. The robot uses AgiBot’s **AGILE** locomotion stack — AgiBot Generative Intelligent Locomotion Engine — which the company says mixes perception with gait decisions on ramps, poles, and uneven bits.

That is the machine story. The table story is that AgiBot finished the five-day Beijing meet with **18 gold, 16 silver, 12 bronze, 46 medals total**, first on both gold and overall boards. Humanoids Daily puts **TianGong** second at **45** (15 gold, 12 silver, 18 bronze) and **Unitree** at **10**. Treat that table as the organizing committee graphic those outlets published, not a PDF I fetched.

## Four production lines, not four prototypes

Gizmochina says AgiBot entered **OmniHand, G2, A3, and X2**, already in mass production or real deployments rather than built only for the Oval. Humanoids Daily’s obstacle still shows a black-and-white X2 with yellow feet picking through yellow ramps and slalom poles.

X2 also took silver and bronze in the 400-meter obstacle, per Gizmochina. **A3** took gold in Tai Chi. **G2** took five golds in scenario work — hotel, library, emergency — the same G2 family AgiBot says is already at **Longcheer** and **SAIC** factories.

## A Human's Take

I care more that X2 ran the obstacles as a shipped config than that AgiBot won the medal table by one over Tiangong. Medal tables mix tweezers, Tai Chi, and hotel rooms. The useful claim is “no special hardware.” If AGILE really transferred from sim onto those yellow ramps without a unique ankle, that is the part worth copying. The next receipt is the same robot on a factory floor that is not painted blue.
