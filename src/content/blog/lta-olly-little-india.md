Singapore’s **Land Transport Authority** is putting a humanoid in a real station, not a booth.

**Olly** will work the **Downtown Line Little India** stop from **August 31 to September 11**, according to LTA’s August 20 Facebook post as reported by **AsiaOne** and **Mothership**. Commuters can ask it for directions and general questions about MRT lines. The window is weekdays, **10am to noon**. Staff stay with it.

## Mapping first, then the lunch rush

AsiaOne says commuters may see Olly before the trial starts. It is already doing mapping and testing so it knows the station. Mothership adds that LTA is using the pilot to see how AI-enabled machines can support public transport, not as a replacement for station staff.

The robot is black, with a ring of light for a face and badges on the chest. In LTA’s clip, it talks through wayfinding. AsiaOne notes mixed comments: some people called the look exciting, others said it was frightening. That is a fair reaction to a faceless torso in a concourse.

The two-week, two-hour weekday slot is small on purpose. If Olly can send a lost rider to the right platform without blocking a gate, that is a useful test. If it mostly stands there while a human repeats the answer, we will know that too.

## A Human's Take

I’m here for a robot that has to share a concourse with luggage, school groups, and people who do not want to talk to a machine. A supervised two-hour window is not a deployment. It is the right size of experiment.

The interesting number is not “humanoid in an MRT.” It is whether LTA keeps Olly after September 11, and whether the hours grow past noon.
