**B-Human**, the joint team from the University of Bremen and the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (**DFKI**), won both Humanoid Soccer League competitions tied to the second World Humanoid Robot Games in Beijing. EuropaWire carried the DFKI press release on **28 August 2026**.

The fields were **22 by 14 metres**, larger than typical RoboCup pitches, which the release says suited the robots’ speed. RCAP matches were two **10-minute** halves. WHRG matches were two **15-minute** halves.

## The two trophies

**RoboCup Asia-Pacific Beijing Masters (22–26 August):** B-Human went through a group with whIRLwind Amsterdam, Inha-United (South Korea), and Starkit (Russia), scored **40 goals** in three wins, beat the US Badger Bots **15–0** in the semifinal, and beat Leipzig’s **HTWK Robots 9–0** in the final.

**WHRG Humanoid Soccer (knockout):** International teams each drew a Chinese opponent in the round of 16. B-Human beat CAU Mountain&Sea **21–1**, Aurora **21–1**, HyperYe **24–0**, then HTWK again, **13–3**, for the Games title.

That is a season piled on a season. The same release says B-Human already won the RoboCup German Open in March and the RoboCup World Championship in Incheon, South Korea, in July. The team site lists **13** world titles and **16** regional wins across the old Standard Platform / Humanoid Soccer line.

Scientific leads named in the release: **Dr. Thomas Röfer** (DFKI) and **Dr. Tim Laue** (University of Bremen), under **Prof. Rolf Drechsler**. CONTACT Software is listed as main sponsor since 2017.

This is a different league from the large 5-a-side humanoids (Tsinghua Huoshen, Booster T2 bodies) that also played in the Oval. B-Human’s robots are the knee-high, camera-headed class the team has run for years in RoboCup.

## A Human's Take

Beating HTWK 9–0 and then 13–3 on a bigger pitch is not a cute exhibition scoreline. It is a software stack that found the ball and the goal for 15-minute halves.
