San Francisco just got another way to book a cleaner that is not a person with a cart. **Tau Robotics** is launching a humanoid cleaning service at **$30 per hour**, with access **invite-only** while the company scales. Founder and CEO **Alexander Koch** announced the rollout on X, with footage he said is shown at **1× speed**.

The company site is blunt about how it works: each humanoid is **jointly controlled by a human operator and AI**. This is not “push a button and leave the robot unsupervised for the day.” It is a live service with a remote operator in the loop.

## What You Can Book

Tau lists three service types on [tau-robotics.com](https://www.tau-robotics.com/):

- **House cleaning**
- **Deep cleaning**
- **Office cleaning**

Capabilities called out include wiping counters and worktops, vacuuming floors (including edges), taking out trash, and clearing clutter from floors.

Booking flow details (from the company book page):

- Every visit is a **one-hour clean** for a **$30 flat** fee, paid up front
- **Bathrooms and bedrooms are off-limits by default**; customers can opt in to those rooms
- Optional notes for other off-limit areas or objects
- **Invite code required** for the San Francisco address; no code means waitlist

Koch’s post: join the waitlist at tau-robotics.com if you do not have an invite yet.

## Why It’s Interesting

A lot of humanoid coverage is factory graphs and billion-dollar rounds. Tau is selling a **price**, a **city**, and a **service menu** — the same shape as early gig apps, with robots (and operators) in the middle.

That honesty about joint human-AI control matters. It keeps the story grounded: the cool part is a humanoid in a real kitchen; the practical part is still a person helping steer.

## A Human's Take

Thirty dollars an hour is a number you can argue about over coffee, which is exactly why this lands. It is early, invite-only, and operator-assisted — and still more concrete than most “coming to homes someday” decks. Watch how they handle privacy, access, and what happens when a glass cabinet is not marked off-limits. If Tau can keep jobs thorough and the operator model quiet, SF gets a weird, fun new service category.
