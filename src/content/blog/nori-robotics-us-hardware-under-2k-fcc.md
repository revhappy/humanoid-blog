A day after the **FCC** put **foreign-produced advanced robotic devices** — humanoids and quadrupeds among them — on its **Covered List**, **Nori Robotics** founder **Antonio Sitong Li** went public with a blunt offer: **US-built mobile bimanual robots at manufacturing cost under $2,000**, plus an open-source SDK, teleoperation software, demonstration data, and engineering support for US labs, researchers, and startups.

That is not a national press release from a mega-lab. It is a YC-backed hardware company reading the same regulatory shockwave as everyone else — and trying to turn domestic assembly into a sales pitch.

## The Offer (From the Founder Thread)

In a July 29 X thread quoting FCC Chairman Brendan Carr’s Covered List post, Li said Nori wants to support US robotics work with affordable domestic hardware. Specs he listed:

- **US-built** mobile bimanual robot
- **20 DOF** total (**7+1 DOF per arm**)
- **1 kg** payload per arm
- **Open-source SDK**
- **Teleoperation software + demonstration data**
- Full engineering support
- Price framing: **manufacturing cost, under $2k**

He pointed interested labs to the **@NoriRobotics** account or **Antoniositongli@norirobotics.com**. Company account posts echoed the same “US robotics ecosystem” framing against the FCC action.

Treat the thread as a **commercial offer**, not an FCC exemption. Covered List rules still govern who can authorize new foreign SKUs; Nori is arguing buyers should not have to wait on that path if a domestic machine exists at appliance-adjacent money.

## What Nori Actually Sells on the Site

Nori’s product page (fetched this session) is more concrete on consumer SKUs than the “under $2k lab offer” post:

| Model | Height | Lift | Price (site) |
|-------|--------|------|----------------|
| **NORI L2** | 100 cm | 650 mm dual Z-axis | **$1,288** pre-order |
| **NORI L2 Grande** | 130 cm | 950 mm dual Z-axis | **$1,322** pre-order |

Both are **wheeled bimanual** machines — not bipeds. Marketing targets household tasks (dishes, coffee, laundry, collaborative bed-making) with a skills marketplace and remote teleoperation. The site says **made in the US / SF**, **ships summer 2026**, with monthly updates promised.

## Who They Are

**Y Combinator** lists **Nori** in the **Summer 2026** batch as a four-person hard-tech / robotics company manufacturing humanoid (form-factor) robots, tagline “Sub-1500$ robot anyone can program,” website norirobotics.com, founder **Antonio Sitong Li** (Columbia CS + Architecture background). YC’s public company page places the team size at **4** and HQ as **New York City**, while the product site stresses San Francisco manufacturing — a dual footprint worth watching as they scale.

Secondary coverage (RuntimeWire) reported an **L3** preorder message and free upgrades for L2 buyers around the same week; the live site we fetched still led with **L2 / L2 Grande** pricing. We stick to what the company site and founder posts state for this article.

## Why This Lands After the Covered List

Mechafeed already covered the July 28 FCC update: new **foreign-produced** advanced robots face equipment-authorization blocks, with Conditional Approval paths via national-security agencies. Existing authorized units are a different problem than importing the *next* SKU.

Cheap Chinese arms and dogs have been the default shopping cart for many US research groups. If new foreign models get harder to authorize, the market splits: pay the exemption/politics cost, or buy domestic. Nori’s bet is that **$1.3k list price and “under $2k at cost for labs”** is close enough to the old import price to matter — *if* the hardware actually works a shift and the SDK is real.

## A Human's Take

I’m so here for boring, buyable US hardware — as long as the DOF count isn’t doing all the selling.

Sub-$2k dual-arm platforms are how students and small labs stop waiting for a grant large enough for an industrial arm. Nori is tiny (YC says four people). That is a feature for focus and a risk for support load if half the country’s robotics clubs email them in the same week.

What I care about next: who receives a unit, what tasks run without constant teleop, and whether “made in SF” holds when they try to ship volume. Policy just raised the value of a domestic BOM. Receipts still have to come from the workbench.
