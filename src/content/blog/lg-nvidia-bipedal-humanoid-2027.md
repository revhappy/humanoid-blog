LG says it is building a next-generation **bipedal humanoid** on **NVIDIA Isaac GR00T**, with a public unveiling targeted for the **first quarter of 2027**. The announcement landed with a memorandum of understanding signed **August 13** at NVIDIA’s Santa Clara headquarters. LG Corp chairman **Kwang Mo Koo** and NVIDIA founder **Jensen Huang** were both there.

The robot is not a finished product. It is a stack commitment: **Jetson Thor** for onboard compute, **Isaac GR00T** as the open humanoid foundation model, and **Halos for Robotics** as the safety layer. Hardware is supposed to come from inside the group — actuators from LG Electronics, sensors from LG Innotek, batteries from LG Energy Solution.

## What they actually signed

LG’s own release frames the deal as a move from “strategic blueprints into full execution” across robotics, AI factories, and mobility. A joint task force of technical and business people is supposed to cover R&D, site validation, and commercialization.

The nearer robot is not the biped. **This year**, LG plans to put its wheeled **CLOiD** on a washing-machine line at LG Electronics’ **Tennessee** plant. Results from that proof of concept are meant to feed later deployments in factories, homes, and commercial spaces.

A robot data factory sits in the middle. LG CNS’s **PhysicalWorks** platform is supposed to collect, synthesize, train, and verify robot data on site. That stream is also meant to feed an in-house **Robot Foundation Model** LG says it is already developing, while still using GR00T.

> “The defining opportunity of physical AI is to give every machine the ability to understand the real world, reason and act safely alongside people,” Huang said in the LG release.

> “As our discussions have moved fast, our tasks and goals in AI Factory and Physical AI have become clear,” Koo said.

## The rest of the package

The same MOU covers more than legs. LG plans a **Vera Rubin** AI-factory reference site in the **first half of 2027**, then an **80-megawatt** facility in **Cheonan** in the **first half of 2028**. Aju Press, citing the company, says the Cheonan site will use prefabricated modules and is supposed to cut construction time by more than **20 percent**. Mobility work is slated around **NVIDIA DRIVE Hyperion** and LG’s in-vehicle software.

## A Human's Take

I will watch Tennessee, not Santa Clara. A signed MOU and a boxed desk robot do not tell you if a biped can hold a shift. CLOiD on a washer line is the first place LG has to show that its own actuators, sensors, and data loop survive grease and cycle time. If that wheeled pilot is late or stays a photo-op, the 2027 humanoid date is just a calendar entry.
