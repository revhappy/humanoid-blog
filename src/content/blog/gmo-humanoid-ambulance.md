If the robot that is supposed to work a shift falls over, someone still has to go get it. **GMO AI & Robotics Corp. (GMO AIR)** is answering that with a van it calls the **GMO Humanoid Ambulance**.

The company announced the vehicle on **September 8, 2026**, and said it will start using it in **Tokyo in September**. GMO AIR, part of **GMO Internet Group**, calls it Japan’s first maintenance van designed for humanoids, based on its own web survey as of that date.

## What is in the van

The official release is specific about the job, not the model list. On a call, the van is supposed to:

- Carry **diagnostic gear, major parts, and repair tools**
- Bring a **humanoid engineer** from **GMO Humanoid Lab Shibuya Showroom**, the group’s physical-AI site
- **Swap in a spare robot** if the unit cannot be fixed on site, then haul the broken one back to the lab

Dispatch is GMO AIR’s own equipment. The company says it will decide case by case, for robots it has introduced. **Tomohiro Uchida**, GMO AIR’s president, said humanoids will not stick if there is no way to keep them running after a fault.

**Euronews** quotes engineer **Shota Takizawa**: the ambulance “carries the replacement humanoid to the site and replaces it with the damaged humanoid.” Uchida told that outlet the idea came from watching damaged units leave a site and take the work with them.

There is **one vehicle**, based at GMO’s **Tokyo headquarters**. Euronews says GMO could add vans and bases if demand grows. Interior and exterior styling is credited to **Yasumichi Morita** of **GLAMOROUS**. The press photos show a white van with red chevrons, GMO marks, and a roof light bar.

## Why it exists

GMO AIR was set up **June 18, 2024** as an AI and robot trading house. **Kyodo News** notes the parent group already sells Unitree-class humanoids in Japan and, with **Japan Airlines**, started a Haneda luggage trial in **May**. This van is the after-sales layer: not a new body, a spare in the driveway.

Kyodo’s launch photos include the van interior — a stretcher-style bed, workbench, and wall-mounted board — and a stage shot of GMO-sold humanoids. **Humanoids Guide** summarized the Euronews piece on **September 12**.

## A Human's Take

I like the spare-bot more than the siren paint. A humanoid that cannot be swapped is a science-fair piece, not a shift worker. One van in Tokyo is a prototype of a service, not a national fleet. Watch whether GMO actually rolls it to a customer site, or whether it stays a showroom prop with purple lights.
