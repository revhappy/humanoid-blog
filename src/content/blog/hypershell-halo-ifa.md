Hypershell unveiled **Halo** at IFA 2026 in Berlin on **3 September**: a consumer exoskeleton that powers **both hips and both knees** as one system. The company calls it the first full-leg unit that coordinates four powered joints that way.

The Origin Edition, limited to the **first 200** units, is on pre-order from **$2,299** / €2,299 / £2,199. PR Newswire says official sales and phased deliveries begin in **November**. Notebookcheck, citing the product page, reports shipping starting **November 2027**. I am keeping both, because the two cited pages do not agree on the year.

## Four motors, one load path

CEO **Kelvin Sun** said in the release they did not bolt knees onto the existing hip exo:

> "We didn't simply add powered knees to an existing hip exoskeleton. We redesigned the architecture, power system, and intelligence around the entire lower body, so four powered joints can move as one with the user."

PR Newswire lists the mechanical bits:

- **HyperDrive Pro**: four motors, **1,490 W** peak
- Knee power unit mass: **170 g** each
- **PocketVolt** battery: **370 g**, **72 Wh**, up to **20 km** in Eco
- **Fish-Spine Adaptive Joint**: up to **198°** lateral, **167°** front-to-back
- More than **30** sensors; HyperIntuition 2.0 trained on over **10,000 hours** of motion data
- Intent prediction about **200 ms** ahead; assistance updates up to **2,500** times per second
- Company-tested squat result: up to **40%** less knee-muscle fatigue
- IP54, folds under **8 L**, five-buckle fit, on in about **20 s**, off in as little as **5 s**

Mass is the other disagreement. PR Newswire says **2.6 kg**. Notebookcheck reports **3.2 kg (7.05 lb)**. Use the company’s 2.6 kg as the press number and treat 3.2 kg as the independent recap until Hypershell publishes a single weighed figure.

Notebookcheck also reports company lab numbers for sit-to-stand (**40%** fatigue cut), cycling (**39%** less exertion, **42%** lower heart rate), and a **1.5-hour** battery charge. Halo includes **Shelly**, a voice/text assistant for setup, assistance tweaks, and route planning. Notebookcheck notes the spec sheet does not say whether Shelly works offline.

## A Human's Take

Powered knees are the interesting part. Hip-only consumer shells already exist; the question is whether four motors stay light enough that you still put the thing on for a real trail. I will believe the 2.6 kg number when someone weighs a production unit with the battery on the hip, and I will believe the ship date when the first Origin serial actually leaves the building. Until then, this is a well-photographed pre-order with a year-shaped question mark.
