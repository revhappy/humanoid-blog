**Aviation Industry Corporation of China** brought a humanoid to WRC that looks like it was designed by people who already make landing gear.

**Global Times** reporter **Feng Fan** saw **Fengxing-001** at the AVIC booth on **August 19**. Staff said the robot stands about **1.8 meters** and weighs **80 kilograms**. Development started in **2024**. The job list on AVIC documents: intelligent aviation manufacturing, ground support, and cross-domain air-ground work. Staff also named aircraft missile loading and, separately, aircraft operation.

## Hybrid joints, modest jog

AVIC described an electro-hydraulic hybrid drive and said it applied aviation power-by-wire to high-output joints. Legs use compact motors derived from aero-engine tech, staff told the paper, to raise load.

Numbers from AVIC, via Global Times:

- jump **30 cm**
- jog **3 km/h**
- walk while carrying **20 kg**
- handle loads up to **15 kg**

On missile loading, staff said two or three robots could do a job that now takes four to six people.

The pilot claim is the one that needs a long look. Staff said a version with the lower body removed has completed simulated-environment trials and can operate an aircraft from instructions. They argued that converting an older airframe to unmanned usually means ripping out wiring and computers, while a modified humanoid would cost about **one-tenth** as much. That is an AVIC talking point, not a flight-test report.

## Next to it: dogs with nets

The same Global Times piece covers the security corner of the hall. **China Electronics Technology Group Corporation** showed a camera-equipped robotic dog for plant patrols. **NEXWISE** demoed an electromagnetic net launcher on a dog: lock a target, fire a restraining net from **5 to 10 meters**, dual-shot with a rear reload. Staff said it is quieter than a powder-powered net gun. The workflow they described is dog IDs the target, operator fires the net, humans handle the rest.

**CGTN**’s WRC wrap the next day also flags robotic dogs for mining and a separate **4-meter** electro-hydraulic giant. That 4-meter machine is not Fengxing-001. Do not mix the two.

## A Human's Take

A 3 km/h jog and a 20 kg carry are not circus numbers. They are closer to a person walking a tool bag. The overhead rig in the photo is the honest part of the booth: this thing is still being held up.

The aircraft-pilot story is a cost argument, not a certification. I will believe it when someone publishes the cockpit, the aircraft type, and who still sits in the jump seat.
