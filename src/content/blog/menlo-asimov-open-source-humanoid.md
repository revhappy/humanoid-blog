Most humanoids are closed platforms with a waitlist and a NDA. **Menlo Research** is shipping the opposite thesis: publish the hardware and let builders assemble it.

**Asimov 1**, listed on [menlo.ai/asimov](https://menlo.ai/asimov) and the product page [menlo.ai/asimov-1](https://menlo.ai/asimov-1), is an educational biped aimed at whole-body control and locomotion training. Company materials date the full-body open release to **27 April 2026** (after **Asimov 0** legs on **27 January 2026**).

## What Menlo publishes

From the Asimov 1 product page (primary source for numbers):

- **Size / mass**: **1.2 m**, **35 kg**
- **Actuation**: **25+2 DoF** (company marketing string on the page)
- **Build kit**: **$20,000** kit path (listed as ~**$11,000** under self-source), with final price varying by tariffs and shipping; **$499** deposit via Stripe
- **Self-source**: full BOM path quoted around **$31,000** depending on suppliers
- **Build time**: expect **100+ hours** of assembly
- **Open by design**: assemble, customize, repair, run your own agents; Digital Asimov browser explorer at try.menlo.ai for zero-hardware trials
- **Roadmap**: Asimov 2 described as a “deployment-ready bimanual” still “coming soon”; long-term goal framed as SOTA by Asimov 5 via open hardware + partner manufacturing

Earlier secondary coverage (e.g. open-source kit write-ups around the April release) described GitHub mechanical files, MuJoCo sim, and DIY kits in the mid-teens of thousands of dollars. **Use Menlo’s live product page for current kit pricing** — the company now lists **$20k** kit / **$31k** self-source, not the older $15k goal posts.

## Why it sits in the build lane

This is not a factory-unit earnings story. It is a reference humanoid for people who want locomotion and whole-body stacks on metal they can open with a hex key. Distributed manufacturing partners and open docs are the business model Menlo is selling — not a home robot subscription.

## A Human's Take

I’ll take a $20k build-it-yourself biped with a public BOM over a $2M pilot NDA that hides the actuator map. The hard part is still the same: 100 hours of assembly only pays off if the walking policy and repair loop are real. Show me community builds that stand after week two, not just orange-backdrop product shots. Open hardware is the right default. Shift-ready is a higher bar.
