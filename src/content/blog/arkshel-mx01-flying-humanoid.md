**Arkshel Robotics** posted test footage of **MX01**, a prototype it calls the first flying transformable humanoid. The company’s English news page is dated **12 August 2026**. The same release carries a Shenzhen dateline of **17 July 2026**, the day Arkshel says it officially launched.

The About page says the firm was **founded in Shenzhen in 2026** and is building transformable, high-payload humanoids. It lists **Prof. Jian S. Dai** as a key academic supporter.

## What the prototype is supposed to do

The launch note describes three working modes on one platform:

- **Humanoid**: meant for human-centered spaces and dexterous work.
- **Quadruped**: more terrain mobility and larger payload, according to the company.
- **Aerial**: a flight module for obstacles, fast reach, and inspection.

A **wheeled** mode is listed as under development for open, structured ground. The company says these are not three bolted-on gadgets. It describes coordinated changes in structure and motion control.

## Who is behind it

Arkshel’s About page places the office in **Nanshan District, Shenzhen**, and describes a team drawn from universities including **Shanghai Jiao Tong**, **SUSTech**, **CUHK**, **Purdue**, **Waseda**, and **NUS**. Dai is listed as President of the Robotics Research Institute at SUSTech, Emeritus Professor at King’s College London, and a Fellow of the Royal Academy of Engineering.

The launch note says seed funding is **almost complete**, backed by **CDF Capital** and **Hengqin idc**, with proceeds aimed at core tech, product engineering, and hiring. The site has a **Pre-Order** link. It does not list a public price.

A YouTube recap by **Kalil 4.0** walks through the same launch and names Dai as a shareholder with a metamorphic-mechanisms background. Treat that as secondary commentary on the company’s own footage.

## A Human's Take

I’m here for the mode switch, not the “world’s first” line. Walking, crawling, then getting picked up by a flight pack is a fun mechanical idea if the joints and docking actually survive more than a demo bay. The on-screen prototype disclaimer is the honest part. Show me a timed transform, a payload number, and a price on that pre-order page before I treat this as a product.
