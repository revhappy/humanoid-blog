**REVEL Robotics** published a public site for **Genesis**, a special-purpose humanoid meant to do skilled shop-floor work, and **Neural Gambit**, a forearm sleeve that records how a person actually does the job. The company also posted a YouTube clip of the robots’ first public appearance at **Ironman Hradec Králové**.

REVEL is based in the **Czech Republic**. The site says it was **founded in 2025**, lists **35+** team members, and is hiring **50+** roles.

## Three pieces, one loop

The company does not sell “a general-purpose home bot.” It describes a capture-to-deploy loop:

- **Neural Gambit**: a forearm sleeve that records “the force, movement, and intent behind expert work” and turns one person’s know-how into robot-training episodes.
- **RAI**: the company’s real-time AI, which it says learns from that capture and turns it into perception, decisions, and movement.
- **Genesis**: the humanoid that “puts learned skill to work” on site.

The site’s pitch is blunt: manufacturers have more orders than hands. It quotes **Korn Ferry** (2018) for the labor-shortage numbers on the page. Those figures are not new REVEL data; they are the company citing an old global report.

## How they say they will build it

REVEL claims a **shared robot core** so the tool can change without rebuilding the whole system. It also says **90–95%** of the robot is produced through additive manufacturing, polymer and metal. A later line floats “micro-factories” that print a replacement part near the customer. That last part is framed as future tense.

The company says it started with a question to manufacturers: “What work can you no longer hire for?” Target sectors listed on the page: manufacturing, logistics, warehousing, healthcare, electronics, consumer goods.

## A Human's Take

I like the sleeve more than the visor. If they really record force, muscle, and intent from a person who already knows the job, that is a cleaner data story than another teleop farm. The additive-manufacturing claim is the one I will keep poking: 90–95% printed is a bold factory number, and I want to see a bill of materials, not a hero render. Show me Genesis doing one shift of the work they say they cannot hire for.
