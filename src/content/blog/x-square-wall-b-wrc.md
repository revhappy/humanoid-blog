X Square Robot livestreamed a logistics cell on **August 12**: its **WALL-B** embodied model driving self-developed **6-axis arms** that pull mixed parcels off an unloading pile, flip labels toward a scanner, and feed a conveyor. The company’s own press release puts the hour at **1,816 parcels** with **over 98%** accuracy.

That is the job called parcel induction. A truck dumps a pile. Someone has to pick, reorient, and belt each package. The pile changes after every grab. Soft packs need flattening. Boxes need squaring. Labels point the wrong way. CEO **Wang Qian** told PR Newswire the question is not one clean pick. It is whether the cell keeps deciding well as the pile mutates, recovers from a miss, and does not stall the rest of the line.

## Then they took the cell to WRC

A second PR Newswire note, dated **August 19**, puts the same station on the World Robot Conference floor at **Booth C107**. The write-up repeats the 1,816/hour, >98% livestream and adds the rest of the booth: a home “day in the life” set, a flower-arranging station that takes a natural-language color request, robot-free data capture with **QUANXTA Zero**, and a dexterous-hand skill demo.

During the show the company also ran a multi-hour sorting livestream. A still from that feed, posted by X Square, shows the cell still working past **five hours**, with the on-screen counter at **9,989** parcels and a **05:13:43** timestamp. I am using that overlay as the sourced number, not a rounded marketing round-up.

## Arms, not a biped, on this task

The hardware here is purpose-built arms and grippers, not a walking humanoid. Interesting Engineering’s short on the August 12 run puts Figure AI’s May cell next to it: three **Figure 03** machines processed **249,560** packages across a **200-hour** run, about **1,250** an hour for the whole cell. Different bet. Figure is selling a general-purpose body that can also climb a ladder. X Square is squeezing seconds out of induction.

The company still shows a humanoid on warehouse work on its site. That is a different body. The WRC sorting numbers belong to the dual-arm cell.

## A Human's Take

If I ran a dock, I would rather have 1,816 honest induction picks than a biped that can also dance. The WRC overlay at five hours is the part I care about: the pile did not win. What I still want is a named customer, a night-shift failure log, and whether the gripper stays cheap when you buy a hundred of them.
