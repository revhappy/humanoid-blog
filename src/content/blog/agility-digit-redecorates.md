**Agility** posted **Innovation at Agility: Digit Redecorates** on **2 September**. Digit, the bird-legged warehouse biped, walks a mock apartment, drags an orange sofa, and shuffles chairs, bags, and other clutter that is not a tote.

The caption is the point: “Every object a different size, weight, and grip. Rearranging a full room’s worth of stuff is the same generalized manipulation Digit uses to take on mixed loads and changing layouts on a real warehouse floor.”

## What the clip actually shows

IEEE Spectrum’s **Evan Ackerman** ran the video as the lead on **4 September** Video Friday. He called the Agility clips a little silly, then said the couch drag is impressive.

  <img src="/images/heroes/agility-digit-redecorates-2.jpg" alt="Digit gripping and dragging an orange sofa in a staged living room" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Digit on the sofa. Source: IEEE Spectrum / Agility.</figcaption>

This is not a home-robot launch. Agility’s own channel frames the room as a stand-in for mixed warehouse loads. Digit still uses gripper-style end effectors, not five-fingered hands. The sofa is light enough that commenters on the YouTube page asked whether someone shoved it from the other side. Ackerman’s read is the one I am using: the interesting bit is Digit walking while hauling an unsecured, awkward payload, not the sitcom set.

## A Human's Take

Dragging a couch is a better test than a tote on a jig. The load is off-center, the feet have to keep walking, and the object is not designed for the gripper. I still want to see it do that for a shift, not a minute of B-roll. If this is the same skill Digit uses when a tote is the wrong size, show me the warehouse version next.
