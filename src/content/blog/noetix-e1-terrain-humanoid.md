Chinese humanoid makers are competing less on flat-floor dance clips and more on **perceptive locomotion**. **Noetix Robotics** is pushing that line with **E1**, the “Enthusiast Vanguard” biped on its English product page — and recent demos of rough ground, a roughly **1 m** platform jump, and stair work have been circulating as the company leans on outdoor-capable motion control.

## What the product page says

From [noetixrobotics.com/en/e1](https://noetixrobotics.com/en/e1):

- **Size / mass**: about **136 cm × 47 cm × 29 cm**, **40 kg**
- **DoF**: **23–28** total (configuration-dependent), including **6 DoF** legs and optional extra wrist/waist freedom
- **Hands**: five-fingered end effectors
- **Payload**: continuous walking carry capacity listed at **≤ 5 kg**
- **Peak joint torque**: up to **150 N·m** on the listed high-load joints
- **Motion claim**: high-difficulty actions including **dancing, jumping, and walking on complex terrains**, with large joint travel (e.g. waist Z ±90°, knee 0–135°)

Pricing on the page is **contact sales** for both E1 and E1 EDU — not a public sticker like Noetix’s smaller **Bumi** consumer SKU.

## Context around the demos

Noetix’s English site and event notes also place E1 in a broader lineup with **N2**, **Bumi**, and **Hobbs** bionics, and they publicize competition results — including an E1 **standing long jump** win with a **1.25 m** score at a World Humanoid Robot Games event. Recent third-party posts describe upgraded motion control for rough terrain and stair climbing; those are leads, not primary specs — the mechanical numbers above come from the company page.

## A Human's Take
