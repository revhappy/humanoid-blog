Tutor Intelligence posted a [16 September product note](https://tutorintelligence.com/blog/announcing-the-next-generation-of-robot-workers-cassie-and-sonny) for the next generation of **Cassie** and **Sonny**. Cassie is the company’s single-arm bulk handler. Sonny is the dual-arm picker it trained in Data Factory 1. Both now ship as mobile workers for brownfield warehouses, not as a lab fleet waiting on a cell.

SiliconANGLE’s [same-day write-up](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/16/tutor-intelligence-launches-second-generation-intelligent-warehouse-robotics-with-a-classroom-to-teach-them/) is blunt about the shape: Cassie is “a giant arm atop a large box with wheels.” Sonny is the one that looks closer to a person because the work is closer to a person’s — bins, SKUs, and shelves.

## Cassie gets a floor

The first Cassie stayed put. The new one drives. Tutor says it is **fully autonomously mobile** on four wheels, with enough battery to finish **more than a shift** without a charge. Stationary jobs such as induction and palletizing now skip a physical install: vacuum generation is onboard. Mobility is the point of the fall work. The company says Cassie is going into **end-to-end case picking** for the first time, billed as B2B fulfillment with **$0 CapEx** and no major facility retrofit.

Tutor still quotes a **~40-day median** from signed paperwork to robots in production. The brain on Cassie is the **Turing** series, which the blog puts at **200 million** parameters. Extra sensing is two **3D LiDAR** units on top of ISO-certified collaborative hardware and 2D LiDAR.

SiliconANGLE, from an interview with co-founder and CEO Josh Gruenstein, adds payload color: Cassie can **lift 50 pounds** and **tug thousands of pounds**.

## Sonny’s first job is each-picking

Sonny is the first Tutor robot the company says will run **Ti0**, a **4.5 billion**-parameter vision-language-action model. Its first assignment is e-commerce fulfillment: navigate a warehouse that already exists, pick arbitrary items from racking, and take completed orders to packout.

The [each-picking product page](https://tutorintelligence.com/each-picking) lists:

- **Up to 100 picks/hour**
- **12-hour** battery
- **10 lb** payload per arm
- Reach heights up to **7 feet**
- Pay for successful picks, with Tutor operating the fleet

Gruenstein told SiliconANGLE the form is a co-design with Cassie: human-like enough for the class of work humans do, not a costume of a person. Tutor’s December **$34 million** Series A and the 100-robot DF1 “classroom” are the data story behind that claim. The new blog says the fleet is growing **20% month over month** and that Tutor is scaling manufacturing in the United States.

## A Human's Take

I care that Cassie can leave the palletizer and drive to the next job without a facilities project. Forty days from paper to production is a number a plant manager can argue with. Sonny’s 100 picks an hour and 10-pound arms are the receipts I want on a live aisle, not a poster. If Ti0 only gets interesting after DF1’s tutors stop holding its hand, say so with a miss rate, not a parameter count.
