Shipbuilding is a terrible first job for a pretty humanoid. Steel, grinders, paint, people in harnesses. **HD Hyundai Robotics** is still aiming a five-finger hand at it.

On **September 11, 2026**, the company said it had put **13 billion won** (about **$9.7 million**) into **AIDIN Robotics**, a force-and-tactile sensor shop in Anyang. Financial News and KED Global both have the stake. KED adds that **Samsung Venture Investment** put in about **$2.2 million** in the same round.

## Sensors first, then a shipyard hand

AIDIN designs capacitive force-torque sensors in-house. Financial News says HD Hyundai wants that stack on its own robot platforms, and that the two firms will **jointly develop a five-finger robotic hand** for shipbuilding and heavy industry, to be mounted on HD Hyundai Robotics' humanoids.

The other joint bet is **surface treatment**: polishing and grinding that people still do on hulls. They want Korean yards first, then overseas yards, including the **United States**.

An HD Hyundai Robotics official called it "a strategic decision to combine the capabilities of both companies and develop robotic solutions based on physical AI." An AIDIN official said the aim is solutions "that can be used in actual industrial settings."

## What AIDIN already ships

The new shipbuilding hand is a joint project, not a SKU with a price. AIDIN's current **AIDIN-Hand** is listed as research-only, with a self-developed **6-axis F/T sensor on a fingertip**, linkage fingers, and power / three-point / pinch grasps. Version **2** was slated for the second quarter of **2026**. A company video for **AIDIN-Hand Gen 2** shows a human-sized hand with tactile sensors holding an orange.

No payload, cycle time, or ship-date for the Hyundai-branded five-finger unit is in today's announcements. The money buys sensors and a design program, not a yard shift.

## A Human's Take

I care about the grinding cell, not the press quote. If a five-finger hand can hold a sander on a curved hull without chewing the coating, Hyundai has a reason to own the sensor company. If this stays a booth hand wrapping tumblers, 13 billion won bought a slide.
