At the World Artificial Intelligence Conference 2026 in Shanghai, AGIBOT unveiled four products on July 18 — and the pitch was not "watch us do parkour." Co-founder, president, and CTO Peng Zhihui put it bluntly: the industry should stop judging embodied AI by whether a robot can nail an impressive demo once, and start asking whether it can be manufactured, delivered, and integrated into real operations.

The headliner is the **AGIBOT A3 Ultra**, a full-size humanoid aimed at long-duration work in commercial and service environments. Company claims are just that — company claims — but the packaging is clearly aimed at buyers who care about shift length and logistics, not Twitter clips.

## A3 Ultra: The Specs AGIBOT Published

According to AGIBOT's own WAIC announcement (and matching secondary coverage from The Robot Report and eWeek):

- **Height / weight**: 1.74 m (about 5.7 ft), 60 kg (about 132 lb)
- **Motion**: 51 active degrees of freedom — the joints that actually articulate
- **Arm payload**: 5 kg per arm
- **Runtime**: up to about eight hours of combined operating time, with support for direct charging, battery swap, and autonomous charging
- **Perception**: 3D LiDAR, RGB-D cameras, fisheye cameras, binocular vision
- **Positioning**: GPS, RTK, and UWB for navigation and multi-robot coordination
- **Compute**: NVIDIA Thor for high-level embodied AI processing

That is a "work a public floor" stack, not a lab-only research platform. Whether those endurance numbers survive a messy customer site is the real test — and AGIBOT has not published third-party validation of the A3 Ultra's shift performance.

## The Rest of the Stack

AGIBOT did not ship one product in isolation:

- **X2 EDU**: ~1.3 m educational/research humanoid with 29 DoF, modular hardware, open interfaces for secondary development
- **G2 Max**: wheeled, force-controlled task robot for material handling and palletizing — heavy industrial work rather than biped theater
- **OmniHand 3 Ultra-M**: 20-DoF direct-drive hand (~630 g) with fingertip vision-based tactile sensing, ~5 kg whole-hand grip load, claimed 0.3 s open/close and ±0.2 mm repeatability

The mix matters. A humanoid for public spaces, a wheeled industrial unit for factories, a hand for contact-rich manipulation, and a modular edu platform for the talent pipeline. That is a portfolio aimed at revenue channels, not a single viral form factor.

## Factory Numbers, With Caveats

AGIBOT used WAIC to highlight industrial work with partners including Longcheer Technology and PIA Automation. At Longcheer's Nanchang tablet quality-inspection line, AGIBOT said G2 robots were integrated into a live production workflow (material collection, placement, equipment interfacing, finished-product return) in 36 hours, supporting about 3,000 units per shift with more than 64 hours of cumulative continuous operation and downtime loss kept below 4%.

Those figures come from AGIBOT. They are useful as a claim of closed-loop production rhythm — not as independently audited KPIs. The company also said more than 60 of its robots operated across WAIC venues for guidance and on-site support, with more than 30 deployed in public areas of the Expo and West Bund venues during the event.

In June 2026, AGIBOT said its 15,000th robot had rolled off the production line — a scale number that is still self-reported, but consistent with a company selling beyond pure humanoid demos.

## A Human's Take

I'm glad someone is talking about uptime and line integration in the same breath as DoF counts. Humanoids that only dance will stay on social media. Humanoids that restock a station or feed a QC cell for eight hours will get purchase orders — or get rejected with hard data either way.

What I want next is not another booth dance. I want customer names who will say, on the record, what the robot costs to run per hour versus the human baseline, how often it needs a human babysitter, and what broke first. Until those answers are public, A3 Ultra is a serious product *announcement*. The shift work still has to prove itself outside Shanghai.
