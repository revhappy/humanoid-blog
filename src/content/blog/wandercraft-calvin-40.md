**Calvin-40** has no head. Cameras sit on the torso. The job, as Wandercraft tells it, is boxes, parcels, tires, panels, and carts — not a smile. On **4 September** the Paris company said it had signed **12** enterprise customers across automotive, logistics, retail, healthcare, and defense.

The [company post](https://www.wandercraft.eu/articles/wandercraft-secures-12-calvin-40-customers-as-it-accelerates-commercial-dominance-in-industrial-humanoids) is the primary. Humanoids Daily followed with the same roster and the robot’s industrial pitch.

## What it is built to lift

Wandercraft started on large material-handling: tires, boxes, parcels, then panels and carts. Picking and sorting are “already being developed and tested,” the post says. Construction and agriculture are named as later outdoor targets.

CEO **Matthieu Masselin**:

> “The question is no longer whether a humanoid robot can perform impressive tasks, but whether it can deliver useful work safely, reliably, and repeatedly at a commercially viable scale.”

And the line they are hanging the product on:

> “Humanoid robots will not scale because they are general. They will become general as they scale.”

The medical side is the claimed foundation: **Atalante X** and **Eve**, with Atalante X in more than **150** rehab centers. Wandercraft says it holds more than **30** patents. Humanoids Daily’s company file describes Calvin-40 as a headless **40 kg**-payload biped; that payload figure is not in the 4 September post itself.

## Renault is still the scale example

Renault Group remains the named industrial partner. **Thierry Charvet**, Renault’s chief industry and quality officer, is quoted on combining Wandercraft’s exoskeleton stack with Renault’s design-to-cost manufacturing. Renault has said it plans to deploy **350** humanoids in the next **18 months**. Humanoids Daily notes the Douai tire-handling line as an early site and **SAPA** as a Tier-1 customer already on the books.

The 12-customer announcement does not name the new logos, publish a price, or give a cycle time.

Humanoids Daily also reports Wandercraft is working with Goldman Sachs on a round near **€100 million** at about **€750 million** pre-money. That is a separate, unconfirmed raise — not part of the customer post.

## A Human's Take

A headless 40-kilo hauler is a more honest industrial sketch than another pretty face. Twelve unnamed customers is a press number until we see a second named floor besides Renault and SAPA. If Calvin can run tires for a shift without a spotter, Masselin’s “general as they scale” line starts to mean something.
