**ROBOTERA** (星动纪元) brought its full-size biped **L7** to the 2026 World Robot Conference in Beijing and used the booth for whole-body teleoperation, not a dance loop.

The company’s English product page lists L7 at **1.71 m** and **65 kg** without the dexterous hand, **55 DoF** with **XHAND1**, a **4 m/s** run, **400 N·m** peak at the knee, **20 kg** dual-arm payload, a **2.1 m** arm span, and compute split as **80 TOPS (x86) + 275 TOPS (Orin)**. Battery: **60 V, 15 Ah**. Binocular cameras and 3D lidar are on the spec sheet.

## The hand on the end

**XHAND 1** is the default five-finger end-effector: **12** active degrees of freedom, **80 N** peak grip, **25 kg** lift with the palm up (**16 kg** palm left), fingertip force **15 N**, and a repeat open-close rate above **2 Hz**. DoNews, covering the WRC booth on 20 August, adds the punch-line demos: pinch a shipping label, run a pipette, drive screws, and click a mouse at **10** times per second.

The same booth showed **XHAND 1 PRO**: **21** active DoF, **18** palm tactile sensors, **0.01 N** stated sensitivity, backlash **≤ ±1 mm**, repeat positioning **±0.1 mm**, and open-close above **5 Hz**.

## The logistics claim

Sci-Tech Innovation Board Daily, via 36Kr on 21 August, says ROBOTERA pitched an embodied logistics stack it calls the first with **product-market-fit** verification. The company told those reporters it runs with **SF Express** and **China Post** in more than **10** logistics centers. Co-founder **Xi Yue** said site-to-site migration dropped from about two months to less than a week, and that customers will pay when takt, accuracy, and hours are numbers they can put in a spreadsheet.

A Reuters dispatch reprinted by *The Indian Express* (20 August) is more specific on units: a sales representative said ROBOTERA had more than **100** parcel-sorting robots in **15** warehouses, with China Post sites running since last year. That Reuters booth note describes a humanoid torso on a wheeled tripod, not the bipedal L7, so treat the warehouse count as the logistics product, not 100 walking L7s.

DoNews also repeats the company line that **9** of the world’s top **10** tech firms by market value are customers, mostly via the wheeled service robot **Q5**.

## A Human's Take

The L7 spec sheet is a real machine: 4 m/s, 55 DoF, a 25 kg palm-up lift on the hand. The interesting claim is the logistics clock, not the rotation jump. I want a shift report from one of those SF or China Post sites, not another booth teleop.
