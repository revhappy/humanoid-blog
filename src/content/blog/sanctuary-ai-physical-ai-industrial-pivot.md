**Sanctuary AI** says it hit production-grade numbers on a hard automotive task — and is no longer waiting for humanoid hardware to go mass-market before shipping **Physical AI**. In a **June 17, 2026** announcement, the Vancouver company reported a **99.5%+** task success rate at a **2.54-second** cycle time on a complex **wire-plugging** job with a global **Tier 1** automotive supplier, validated against the customer’s live production benchmarks.

## Hardware-agnostic, on purpose

The strategy shift is explicit. Rather than waiting for humanoid platforms to reach mass commercialization, Sanctuary says it is deploying Physical AI on **existing commercial robotic platforms** — a hardware-agnostic path that still aims to support future industrial humanoids.

**Olivia Norton**, co-founder and CTO, put the bar in customer language:

> “Physical AI adoption is gated by AI that meets both performance and cycle time requirements. That’s what customers are seeking, and that’s what we are delivering.”

The proof-of-concept task: plug insertion with **flexible materials** that shift while moving on a **conveyor**. Sanctuary calls matching live-line throughput on that class of contact-rich work a world-first against industrial benchmarks, and published video of the system at full line speed.

## What secondary coverage adds

**Techcouver** described the move as evolving Sanctuary’s model to offer software for other robotics platforms. **The Robot Report** covered the same validation as performance at a Tier 1 automotive supplier and noted Sanctuary is applying physical AI without waiting for humanoids.

Sanctuary’s own about-line still mentions proprietary **hydraulic hands** and IP in the long stack, but the June message is clear: sell production-ready performance **now** on robots factories already own.
