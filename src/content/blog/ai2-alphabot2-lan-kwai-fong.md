**AI² Robotics** put its wheeled **AlphaBot 2** (“Aibao”) on a bar in Hong Kong’s **Lan Kwai Fong** on **31 August 2026**. *Sing Tao Daily* photographed the machine pulling a tap while a crowd watched from the rail. The company frames it as Hong Kong’s first service robot in a real open nightlife space, not a booth.

Lan Kwai Fong Group chairman **Allan Zeman** told *Sing Tao* the robot should start serving customers in about **two weeks**. Guests would order on a tablet; the robot would pick a glass and pour. He also said the unit is meant to stay in the district, not tour and leave.

## What the bar actually asked for

Zeman’s quote in the 31 August piece is dry: introducing a robot is “why not.” He said AI² came to Lan Kwai Fong, and the two sides spent about **a month and a half** getting ready. Languages on the robot, per both *Sing Tao* stories: Mandarin, English, Cantonese, and other languages for a tourist street.

AI² founder and CEO **Guo Yandong** told the paper the company pretrains a “common-sense” robot brain and likened it to a child. He said the stack can pick up roughly **one new skill a month**. Hong Kong, he added, is why they wired in extra languages.

The company press line, carried by IT Home and *Huanqiu*, is more industrial: hardware export, customs, a local drink-selling permit, and a paid service setup. Treat that as AI²’s claim. *Sing Tao*’s on-the-ground reporting is the timeline that matters: photo day 31 August, service about two weeks later.

## NeuroVLA in a noisy room

AI²’s **NeuroVLA** model is the control story they want attached to the pour. The company describes a three-layer split it maps onto cortex, cerebellum, and spinal cord: language and task planning, high-rate motion correction, and a fast safety reflex. Lab numbers in the same brief: motion jitter down **more than 75%**, a collision reflex in **20 milliseconds**, and about **0.4 watts** for that reflex path.

Those figures are company experiment claims, not a third-party bar audit. The point they are selling is that a nightlife floor is a worse test than a cube kiosk. AI² already runs **AlphaBot Cube** drink stations in Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, and other Chinese cities, with robots making coffee, ice cream, and matcha. Lan Kwai Fong is the jump from a modular booth to a street that does not pause for a demo script.

## A Human's Take

A robot that can pull a tap in Central is a better picture than another warehouse tote video. I still want the two-week mark: does it take tablet tickets through a Friday night, or is this a photo call with a pour. Zeman is buying a crowd magnet as much as a bartender. If Guo’s “one skill a month” holds after the neon and the elbows, the interesting sequel is not more languages. It is whether the same wheeled upper body can restock the back bar without a handler hovering.
