Hands are still the unglamorous boss fight of humanoids. **Proception** is trying to sell that boss fight as a product.

TechCrunch reports the Mountain View startup raised an **$11 million seed** led by **First Round Capital**, with **Y Combinator** and **BoxGroup**. Founder **Jay Li** — formerly a technical lead on Tesla’s **Optimus** program — settled a trade-secret lawsuit with Tesla (suit dismissed earlier in the month of the announcement). Tesla did not comment to TechCrunch.

## What They’re Building

Proception says it is shipping the first batch of a **high-dexterity robotic hand** to researchers and robotics companies, with wider orders opening.

Per TechCrunch and company description:

- Hand with **22 degrees of freedom** and multiple joints per finger
- **ProGlove**-style sensor gloves: humans wear gloves (and a headset) to capture hand interaction data **without a robot in the loop**
- The same glove stack is meant to act as the hand’s “skin” for sensing
- Goal: become a **hand supplier** so other robot makers do not have to invent dexterity from zero

Li’s critique of pure teleoperation: the operator often lacks touch feedback, and you only scale as fast as your robot fleet. Gloves-for-data is his answer for volume and fidelity.

First Round’s **Bill Trenchard** told TechCrunch dexterous manipulation is “the last mile” for humanoids that actually perform — and that Proception’s hardware-plus-data combo was a big reason to invest.

## A Human's Take

Full-body demos get the views; hands decide whether a humanoid can load a dishwasher without becoming a meme. Proception is a classic small-team bet: own the hard subsystem, sell to everyone else. The Tesla history is noise for the internet and signal for grit. The real scoreboard is simple — who reorders hands after the first batch, and whether glove-scale data actually beats teleop-only training.
