Samsung Electronics ran a **closed-door humanoid demonstration** on **August 10** at its **Umyeon R&D campus** in Seocho, Seoul, according to *Seoul Economic Daily*. **Roh Tae-moon**, who heads the Device eXperience division and oversees the new **Robotics eXperience (RX)** unit, attended in person.

The prototype itself is not in the photos. SEDAILY illustrated the story with **Bot Handy**, the household arm Samsung showed in **2022**. That is the machine readers can actually see.

## What sources say happened in the room

Industry sources told SEDAILY the unreleased humanoid handled **precise finger work**, including **operating a camera to take photographs**. The paper calls that a high-difficulty contact task: joints have to move finely while the force at the shutter stays under control.

Samsung has not posted official stills, a spec sheet, or a public video of that machine. Treat the camera claim as a sourced report, not a measured lab result.

*TrendForce*, citing *ZDNet Korea*, says Samsung has been building an in-house humanoid **separate from Rainbow Robotics**, already at an advanced stage, and that the company is adapting **home-appliance motor tech** into **actuators**. The same report says Samsung can pull factory data from appliances, phones, semiconductors, and displays, and that it has its own large language model, **Samsung Gauss**.

Samsung has **not publicly confirmed** the confidential in-house program, *TechTimes* notes.

## RX first, then the factory

SEDAILY says foreign robotics hires recruited through the **Future Robotics Initiative Group** in March have started work at Umyeon, covering hands, manipulation AI, and walking/full-body control. Internal job-posting winners are expected to land in RX teams soon.

The commercial plan, as those sources describe it, is **B2B first**: run robots at Samsung affiliate plants, collect work data, check safety, then talk about consumer machines. Umyeon does R&D. The **Gumi** plant is the data side. TrendForce adds a planned **Data Factory** at Gumi that would fold existing process data into robot training.

SEDAILY also flags a group stack that is still a plan, not a shipped robot: **Samsung Foundry** for AI chips, **Samsung SDI** solid-state cells as a candidate battery, and fall **smart glasses** as a first-person video source for physical AI.
