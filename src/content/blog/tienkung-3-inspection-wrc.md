Day two of the **World Robot Conference** in Beijing put a humanoid in front of a bank of high-voltage cabinets, not a boxing ring.

**Global Times** photographed **Tien Kung 3.0** at the **X-Humanoid** booth on **August 20**, holding a tester against the gear. A company representative told the paper the stack pairs X-Humanoid’s in-house **HuiSi KaiWu** embodied-intelligence platform with **State Grid**’s large language model. The pitch: read an operation ticket, interpret the job, walk the aisle, inspect the cabinet. The same representative said there are already benchmark deployments in several provinces.

## The rest of the floor was jobs, not dances

The same Global Times dispatch, tied to a **2026 Humanoid Robot Industry Development Report** released Thursday at the show, is full of shipment totals. I am parking those. The machines on the floor are the story.

At the **National and Local Co-built Humanoid Robotics Innovation Center** booth, a firefighting **LingLong L2.0** made its first public run: shut a gas valve, cut power, aim an extinguisher, no remote. A **WeiLong M2** handled casualty transfers. A **XunLong** quadruped searched simulated rubble.

**Robot++**, from Zhejiang, showed wall-climbers that stick to carbon steel with permanent magnets, grind weld coatings, then run magnetic-particle and ultrasonic tests. Engineer **Zhao Dekui** told Global Times that inspecting a **3,000 cubic meter** spherical tank takes workers **15 days to a month** with scaffolding. The robots finish grind-plus-inspect in **four days** without it. One unit covers curvature down to a **2.5-meter** diameter. Another mounts a humanoid upper body on the climbing chassis and swaps end tools.

**CGTN**, covering the same conference on August 20, puts the show at more than **300** companies (**69%** more than last year) and more than **3,000** exhibits, including more than **300** debut products. It also describes a **1.35-meter, 39-kilogram** **Tiangong Omni** climbing plum-blossom piles and stairs, plus a separate **4-meter** electro-hydraulic giant aimed at nuclear, deep-sea, and heavy-load work. Those are different machines from Tien Kung 3.0.

Tien Kung 3.0 itself is not a new name. X-Humanoid launched the platform in **February**. What is new this week is the inspection cell on the show floor, wired to a utility model, with a company saying it already has provincial sites.

## A Human's Take

I will take a probe on a live cabinet over another kung-fu loop. Reading a work ticket is the part that usually dies in a demo: the robot has to know *which* switch, not just how to stand.

“Several provinces” is still a phrase. The next receipt is a named substation and a shift count, not a booth crowd filming the cabinet row.
