**Holiday Robotics** just put serious money behind a very un-sci-fi idea: a **wheeled factory humanoid** that is mostly about **hands**. The Seoul startup, founded in **2024**, raised **$105 million (KRW 155B) Series A** to accelerate **FRIDAY** — its first industrial platform.

**Maeil Business**, as summarized by **The AI Insider**, called it the largest Series A for a South Korean startup and the largest single funding round for a Korean humanoid company. **The Robot Report** and Holiday’s own blog confirm the figure and the industrial focus.

## FRIDAY’s Spec Sheet (What They Published)

From Holiday’s Series A post and Robot Report coverage:

- **Form**: wheel base + dexterous upper body (semi-humanoid)
- **64 degrees of freedom**, with **40 in the hands**
- **Tactile sensing** and force-aware control
- **Hot-swappable batteries** for full-shift work
- Safety hardware: lightweight low-inertia hands, fully backdrivable joints, 360° cameras and lidars

## Full Stack, Not Just a Pretty Arm

Holiday is building software and tooling in-house around what it calls **Vision-Language-Skill (VLS)**:

- **FRIDAY** — the hardware
- **Holiday Sim** — simulation before deploy
- **Holiday Lab** — demos and failures turned into reusable skills
- **OASys** — deployment loop

CEO **Kiyong Song** framed the company around speed to *useful* work, not demo theater: factories need robots that can be put to work quickly. The round pulls in a long list of Korean VCs plus **Goodwater Capital**, with Maeil also naming policy banks among participants. Capital goes to R&D hiring, manufacturing readiness, and U.S. + Korea team growth.
