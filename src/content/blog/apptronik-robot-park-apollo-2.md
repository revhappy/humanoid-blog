Okay so imagine this: a nearly **90,000-square-foot** building in Austin where humanoid robots show up to *work* — logistics drills, manufacturing tasks, retail-style choreography — and every awkward grasp and clean pick becomes training data. That’s **Robot Park**, Apptronik’s flagship data-collection facility, which the company said it expanded and opened in a bigger form on **June 30, 2026**.

And no, this is not “one robot does a dance for a keynote.” Apptronik is talking **fleets of Apollo 2** robots, bipedal *and* wheeled, looping through real customer-style tasks while feeding a research partnership with **Google DeepMind** on Gemini Robotics models.


## What’s Actually New

From Apptronik’s own press release:

- **Robot Park** in Austin is the anchor of a growing network of Robot Parks at customer and partner sites worldwide — with more cities planned
- **Apollo 2** is the current modular humanoid platform (bipedal + wheeled-base configs), already the workhorse at Robot Park for **more than a year**
- Data comes from **teleoperation + autonomous execution**, plus high-fidelity sim
- Partner/customer network called out includes **Google DeepMind**, **Mercedes-Benz**, and **GXO**
- Learnings from Apollo 2 are meant to power the next commercial product: **Apollo 3**

CEO **Jeff Cardenas** put it in very non-hype terms (my favorite kind): the industry spent years on demos; Apptronik wants robots that work every day on the job — a continuous learning loop with DeepMind’s robotics team, real environments, real tasks.

## Why Wheels *and* Legs?

This part made my engineer-brained friends nod and my “cool robot walk” self slightly pout: the **wheeled** Apollo is there because it can line up with **existing industrial mobile-robot safety standards** and drop into customer ops sooner. The **bipedal** version is the chaos agent — more adaptable, more places to practice not face-planting.

Chief Commercial Officer **Barry Phillips** said modular design is a direct response to customer demand for adaptable automation: same core humanoid tech, different lower half depending on the job.

Honestly? That’s the grown-up version of “we can walk” marketing. It’s “we can ship something that compliance will actually sign off on *while* we still train the biped.”

## A Human's Take

I’m a sucker for a good robot video, but Robot Park is the unsexy plot twist I actually care about: **reps over reels**. If Apollo 2 is grinding logistics and factory-ish tasks all day so Gemini Robotics gets better data, that’s how you stop humanoids from being eternal “almost.”

Still — “network of parks” and “Apollo 3 incoming” only matter if customer sites keep the robots online when the cameras leave. I’ll be watching for boring proof: hours logged, task lists that look like real SOPs, and whether wheeled Apollo shows up next to humans without a velvet rope. Until then, consider me cautiously hyped with a hard hat on.
