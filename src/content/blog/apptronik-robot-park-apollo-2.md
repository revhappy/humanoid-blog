Apptronik’s pitch for **Robot Park** is less about a single stage demo and more about volume. On June 30, 2026, the Austin company said it opened a newly expanded flagship facility — nearly **90,000 square feet** — where fleets of **Apollo 2** humanoids run logistics, manufacturing, and retail-style tasks so the data can feed embodied AI models.

Those models sit inside a research partnership with **Google DeepMind** on Gemini Robotics. Apptronik’s line is simple: robots working every day, collecting data, improving with each cycle.

## What’s Actually New

From Apptronik’s press release:

- **Robot Park** in Austin anchors a growing network of similar sites at customer and partner locations, with more cities planned
- **Apollo 2** is the modular platform (bipedal and wheeled-base) already used as the park workhorse for **more than a year**
- Data comes from **teleoperation and autonomous execution**, plus simulation
- Partner and customer sites called out include **Google DeepMind**, **Mercedes-Benz**, and **GXO**
- Learnings from Apollo 2 are meant to power the next commercial product: **Apollo 3**

CEO **Jeff Cardenas** framed it against the industry’s demo habit: years of showing what robots *can* do on stage, versus what they do on the job. Robot Park is the data fuel; Apollo 2 is the machine that makes the loop possible.

## Why Wheels and Legs

The wheeled configuration is designed to line up with existing industrial mobile-robot safety standards so it can drop into customer operations sooner. The bipedal version is for messier spaces and walking practice.

Chief Commercial Officer **Barry Phillips** said modular design answers customer demand for adaptable automation: same core humanoid stack, different lower half depending on the job.

## A Human's Take

This is one of the more honest industrial stories in humanoids right now — not because Apollo is magic, but because Apptronik is measuring progress in **shifts and datasets**, not just viral clips. The open question is whether the Robot Park network keeps units online when the press cycle moves on, and whether Apollo 3 inherits real skills or just more carefully curated demos.

Watch the boring metrics: hours logged, tasks that look like actual SOPs, and wheeled Apollo next to humans without a velvet rope.
