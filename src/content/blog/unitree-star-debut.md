**Unitree** started trading in Shanghai on Wednesday, and the first print was not shy. **Associated Press** reports shares initially soared as much as **629%** on the STAR Market debut. The Hangzhou company raised about **6.1 billion yuan ($904 million)** at **150.80 yuan ($22.36)** a share. As of midday, AP says the stock was still up nearly **500%**.

**Nikkei Asia**, in an updated report the same day, said shares jumped **460%** on the first session.

This is a new chapter from the CSRC registration we covered earlier. Paper approval is not a tape. Today there is a tape.

## What listed, and what it sold last year

AP calls Unitree the first publicly traded humanoid robotics maker in mainland China. Founder **Wang Xingxing** started the firm in **2016** in Hangzhou. Proceeds, the company said, go to advanced robot R&D and a manufacturing base.

**Omdia**, cited by AP: of roughly **15,000** humanoid robots shipped globally last year, Unitree and **AGIBOT** each shipped more than **5,000**.

Unitree reported about **1.7 billion yuan (about $250 million)** in 2025 revenue, mainly humanoids and quadrupeds. More than **40%** of that was overseas. The U.S. was roughly **13%** of revenue last year, before the FCC banned imports of new foreign-made humanoids and quadrupeds. Unitree said the ban applies to its new models. Existing models can still be sold, with the risk that Washington widens the net.

**UBTECH**, listed in Hong Kong, fell **10.6%** on Wednesday, AP reports.

## The other half of the day

The listing landed on the same Wednesday as the **World Robot Conference** in Beijing. AP’s floor story has Unitree robots boxing, dancing, and playing ping pong. That is the show. The exchange is the bid.

Morningstar analyst **Kangyuxiao Li**, quoted by AP:

> “The real competitive test will be whether companies – Chinese or American – can achieve reliable performance and attractive returns on investment in large-scale industrial and commercial deployments.”

Li also said the IPO “gives mainland investors direct exposure to one of the sector’s leading companies.”

## A Human's Take

A **629%** open is a crowd, not a factory. Midday still near **500%**, Nikkei’s **460%** first-day figure, and a **$904 million** raise tell you the tape wanted a humanoid name.

I care whether the 5,000-plus units Omdia counts work a shift after the lockup. The FCC already cut the easy U.S. story for new models. If the manufacturing-base spend in the prospectus turns into robots that survive hour six, the listing will have meant something besides a first-day multiple.
