If a humanoid is a computer that walks, **1X Technologies** just published a better I/O layer. On July 9, 2026 the company unveiled new **tendon-driven hands** for its **NEO** home robot: **25 degrees of freedom**, all force-controlled, and — in the company’s words — **“an API to the physical world.”**

These are the hands 1X says will ship on **every NEO**.

## The Specs That Matter

From the official [NEO’s Hands](https://www.1x.tech/discover/neos-hands) post:

- **25 DoF total** — **22 fully actuated** in fingers and palm, **3 at the wrist**
- **1X Tendon Drive** with quasi-direct-drive ratios of roughly **5:1 to 15:1**
- Joints are **force-controlled and fully backdrivable** (“force transparency”)
- **IP68** sealing with food-safe materials so the robot can work at a sink and wash its hands
- Peak numbers called out: distal flexion forces up to **45 N**, wrist torque **17.75 Nm**, positioning about **±0.2 mm**
- Tactile sensing for **normal force, contact location, and shear** (slip detection)
- Production claim: capacity to build about **10,000 hands** this year on an in-house line; **hundreds** already off the line

CEO **Bernt Børnich** said the goal was hands that match or surpass human capability “across every dimension that matters,” so models are limited by data, not by dumb claws.

## What 1X Shows Them Doing

The company demo list reads like a household skill tree: LEGO builds, screws and coins, light bulbs, screwdrivers, grapes sorted by color, tea poured from a kettle, USB-C plugs, wine glasses, paper towels, even sign language. Whether every clip is fully autonomous is a fair question for any humanoid launch — 1X is clear that the hands are the hardware substrate for learning, not a magic trick by themselves.

The engineering argument is neat: high gear ratios make most robot hands write-only; low-ratio tendons let force flow back through the motors so every push is also a measurement.

## A Human's Take

Calling a hand an “API” is marketing — and also accurate. If NEO is going into kitchens, the boring magic is gentle force control, slip sensing, and a hand you can hose off. Hands at scale matter as much as the flashy grasp videos. Watch whether customer units keep the demo-day finesse after months of drawers, dishes, and dog toys.
