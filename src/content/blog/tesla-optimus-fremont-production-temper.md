**Tesla** is finally showing floor photos of an **Optimus** production line in **Fremont** — and, on the same earnings week, **Elon Musk** is telling investors not to expect a vertical hockey stick.

**Business Insider**’s July 24, 2026 explainer, drawing on the latest earnings call, says Musk offered few details and **tempered expectations** for how quickly production would increase. Gen-3 production is described as beginning **“soon”** in Fremont. Earlier in the year, public coverage had pointed to **late July or August** for limited volume after Model S/X space was cleared — timelines that are now living under a slower-ramp narrative.

## What’s on the Record This Week

From BI’s sourced summary of the call and company communications:

- **Fremont line photos** released; manufacturing expected to begin soon there
- Model **S/X** production at Fremont stopped earlier this year to free capacity for an Optimus line **designed** for up to **a million robots a year** long-term
- A second line under construction in **Austin** is planned for **10 million** annual capacity — capacity planning, not current output
- First bots feed an **“Optimus Academy”** for task practice and data; Musk has spoken of **10,000–30,000** robots refining skills there
- AI chief **Ashok Elluswamy** said training could push capabilities toward a “superhuman level”
- Musk warned the ramp will be **slow** because **many Optimus components lack an established supply chain**

Tesla did not respond to BI’s request for comment on the package.

## Three Problems Musk Named

BI organizes Tesla’s own framing into three hard gates:

1. **Intelligence** — understanding and moving in the physical world; Tesla leans on FSD camera/AI stack and a driving simulator adapted for virtual robots
2. **Hands** — custom motors, gears, sensors for “human and then superhuman dexterity”
3. **Mass production** — early output “agonizingly slow” in prior Musk language; unit cost estimates of about **$20,000–$25,000** only after million-unit scale

Cornell’s **Guy Hoffman** told BI humanoids remain a “fantasy product” near-term and a “very risky bet,” arguing physical autonomy is harder than the automotive analogy implies.

## Competitive Backdrop BI Notes

While Tesla holds Gen-3 under wraps (Musk has said to limit copycats), peers are shipping stories: **Agility** toward a public listing with Digit deployments, **Figure** in logistics, **Sunday**, **1X**, and **Weave** aiming at homes this fall.

## A Human's Take

Photos of a line are not a production rate. The useful sentence from this earnings cycle is the supply-chain one: if there is no mature BOM, the million-unit architecture is a factory *shape*, not a 2026 output plan. I’ll track Fremont unit counts, academy utilization, and whether “soon” turns into serial numbers customers can audit — not another capacity slide.
