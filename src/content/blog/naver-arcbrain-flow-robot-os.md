**Naver** is pushing its robot software stack beyond developer-only tooling. According to [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/technology/2026/07/29/naver-aims-to-be-android-of-robotics-with-new-service), **Naver Labs Europe** built **ARCBRAIN Flow** — a no-code / low-code layer so site operators can design robot services without rewriting the full control stack every time a building layout changes.

## What ARCBRAIN Flow is

ARCBRAIN Flow sits on top of Naver’s large-scale robot control system **ARCBRAIN**. Non-developers can combine building blocks — movement paths, delivery-complete notifications, and other service steps — into site-specific workflows. A Naver official told Seoul Economic Daily the goal is “customized services tailored to customer demand at each site,” with controls “as easy as moving shapes with a mouse in PowerPoint.”

Naver Labs Europe filed a Korean trademark for the name on July 10 and is pursuing European patent procedures, the report says.

## How it fits the ARC stack

Naver has been assembling an **ARC** robot OS ecosystem since founding **Naver Labs** in 2017. Pieces already in the public description:

- **ARCBRAIN** — cloud-based multi-robot control
- **ARC eye** — vision-based positioning without GPS
- **ARC mind** — web-based robot OS (Naver’s claim: first of its kind)

Those tools run on Naver’s second HQ, **1784** in Seongnam, driving the in-house autonomous delivery robot **Rookie**. CEO **Choi Soo-yeon** previously told investors Naver concentrated core capabilities on ARC and considers its robot software among the world’s strongest.

The gap ARCBRAIN Flow targets: outside robot-friendly buildings like 1784, services had to be redesigned from scratch. Flow is meant to make ordinary buildings usable without a full re-engineering pass.

## Where it could ship next

Full-stack ARC deployments are currently limited to **1784** and **Tokyo Midtown Yaesu**, the first export case of Naver’s smart-building model. The company is considering Flow for Yaesu so delivery routing and service rules are easier to tune in a building never designed for robots.

Seoul Economic Daily cites **Global Market Insights** projecting robot software from **$29.6B** this year to **$80.6B** in 2031 and **$185.2B** in 2036 — market-size framing for why a platform play is attractive, not a Naver revenue forecast.

Korea University professor **Lee Sung-yup** flagged the real scoreboard: adoption. Many robot makers ship their own control software; Naver has to prove full-stack verification experience wins multi-vendor sites.
