Not every serious robot comes out of a Series B office park. **Threehalves**, a nearly seven-foot centaur-style machine from Northern California engineer Beau G. and a small garage team under the **Satyress** banner, is built for hazardous outdoor work — modular tools, low center of gravity, and fail-safes that still matter when air pressure dies.

Interesting Engineering’s August 2026 report is the clearest English summary of the design; the project site is [satyress.com](https://www.satyress.com/).

![Threehalves centaur-style robot outdoors with modular saw tool](/images/heroes/threehalves-centaur.jpg)

## Design choices that read as industrial

Threehalves combines a **humanoid upper body** with a **four-legged** base. The point is stability on rubble, soft ash, and slopes where bipeds fight balance. Each joint uses **passive friction** so the machine can hold pose without continuous power while standing. Pneumatics include automatic brakes and pin locks if pressure is lost; exposed chest air reservoirs double as a physical emergency stop anyone nearby can hit without a remote.

Instead of five-finger hands, the arms use **quick-connect tool mounts** that pass **12 V / 18 V / 48 V** power plus pressurized air. Tool swaps (chainsaw, drill, impact gun, industrial screwdriver) are described as minutes-scale field changes. Arms and tool assemblies use three interchangeable sections so damaged segments can be replaced without a depot.

Cameras in the horn assembly provide multi-view feeds (arms, tools, legs) and backup vision if joint sensors fail. Teleoperation is aimed at game-controller / joystick control from a safe distance, including when the robot is out of direct sight.

## Context, not a product SKU yet

IE notes the team has **not** announced pricing, availability, or emergency-service certification status. The same article contrasts a separate Chinese **Run Robotics** “centaur” wheel-leg hybrid shown at WAIC 2026 (100–120 kg typical payload claims, Q4 2026 production-line target) — a different company and architecture, useful only as market context.

## A Human's Take

I care more about the friction brakes and the physical kill-valve than the goat horns. Hazardous work is where form-factor diversity still beats biped fashion: if the machine falls over when the compressor hiccups, it is a liability. Until there are published field hours and a real buyer, treat this as a serious prototype with the right instincts, not a fleet product.
