**China North Industries Group Corporation (NORINCO)** is showing a full-size humanoid at the 2026 World Robot Conference that is built to copy a remote operator, not to dance.

Global Times, writing from a NORINCO briefing dated August 18, calls the robot **Fuxi**. It is meant for all-weather reconnaissance, intelligent patrols, and work in hazardous areas. A dedicated teleoperation stack, branded **“external avatar,”** is described as China’s first independently controllable teleop solution for a full-size humanoid around **90 kilograms**.

## What NORINCO says it does

The robot comes from NORINCO’s innovation institute. The company lists three technical pillars: human-motion sensing, motion transfer across robotic platforms, and coordinated whole-body control.

On sentry duty, Fuxi is supposed to identify people and raise alerts when something looks off. Multiple units can run in formation. Each unit, NORINCO says, can reproduce an operator’s movements in real time.

Interesting Engineering, citing the same Global Times report, converts the mass as about **198 pounds**. The company frames the pitch as “remote human control and on-site robotic operations.”

A formation demo at the expo is planned as a nighttime urban inspection: multi-robot coordination, assessment, and closed-loop response, in NORINCO’s words.

## The rest of the booth

NORINCO is leading the central state-owned enterprise hall. Eighteen affiliated units are showing **68** products. Hangzhou Zhiyuan Research Institute is adding ballistic helmets and plates, a mobile edge-cloud box the company calls the first trusted-cloud certified edge-cloud product in China’s defense industry, and AR glasses that fuse low-light and infrared.

Other hardware on the list includes a walking-assistance exoskeleton, a wearable ducted-fan aircraft, a two-seat hybrid aircraft, a towing robot, and a robotic guide dog.

The conference itself, per Beijing figures repeated in the Global Times piece, runs Wednesday through Sunday with more than **2,000** exhibits from over **300** exhibitors and more than **150** debut products.

## A Human's Take

A 90 kg humanoid with a named teleop stack is a serious piece of metal. It is also still a press description plus a formation clip. I want latency numbers, how far the operator can sit, and what happens when the radio dies.

Shield-and-baton choreography is not a patrol shift. If Fuxi can hold a formation at night without a handler in the same room, that is the story. Until then it is a remote puppet with a new name.
