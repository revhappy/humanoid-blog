Not every “humanoid” round is about legs. **AI² Robotics** (also styled **Ai2** / **智平方**) raised about **$735 million** in a fresh financing round that, per **The Robot Report** (July 10, 2026), pushed its valuation past **50 billion RMB — roughly $2.8 billion U.S.**

That puts the Shenzhen company among the larger Chinese physical-AI financings of the year — and squarely in the **wheeled torso** camp rather than the biped arms race.

## What TRR Reports About the Round and Product

- **Investor mix**: state-backed funds (including the National Small and Medium Enterprises Development Fund), corporates from **Sino Biopharmaceutical** to **Moutai Group**, and financial firms such as **CICC Capital** and **GSR Ventures**
- **Hardware**: wheeled mobile manipulator with humanoid upper body and five-fingered hands; **34+ degrees of freedom** and a custom waist-leg lift that raises the torso without bipedal walking
- **Tradeoff TRR highlights**: no stairs or rough terrain, but lower cost, higher mechanical durability, and fewer public-space regulatory issues than walkers
- **Software**: proprietary **Alpha Brain** vision-language-action (VLA) model for spatial reasoning and multi-step planning
- **Deployment target**: **AlphaBot 2** into logistics, manufacturing, biotech, public service, and retail — not a near-term home product pitch

The company’s English site positions AI² as a general-purpose robot developer/manufacturer and points developers to an **AlphaBrain** platform. TRR links the company site as [ai2robotics.com/en](https://ai2robotics.com/en/).

## Why the Form Factor Story Matters

China’s humanoid boom is often pictured as biped fleets. AI² is a high-dollar reminder that **wheel + humanoid torso + hands** is a commercial design choice: more like a mobile dual-arm station that can still work existing aisles. Holiday Robotics’ FRIDAY and Ultra’s stationary OP1 are cousins of the same pragmatism — different countries, same allergy to falling over on a customer floor.
