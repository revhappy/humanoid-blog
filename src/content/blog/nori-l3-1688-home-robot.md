Most “home humanoid” price tags still sit in industrial territory. **Nori Robotics**, the YC-backed team behind the earlier **L2** pitch, now fronts **Nori L3** on its site as **“the most capable robot for $1,688”** — assembled in **San Francisco**, **$200** deposit, **ships Fall 2026**.

## Specs on the product page

From [norirobotics.com](https://www.norirobotics.com/):

- **20 degrees of freedom**, **1.5 kg** payload **per arm**  
- **LiDAR**: **12 m** range, **8–12 Hz**, **0.72°** angular resolution at **10 Hz**  
- **Cameras**: **×4 720p RGB**, up to **30 fps**, on grippers, head, and neck  
- **Audio**: speaker and mic for spoken commands  
- **Battery**: **6–8 hours**  
- **App**: “Nori Lab” laptop app to train, operate, and manage  

Listed everyday tasks include fetching from the fridge, pouring a drink, loading dishes, and folding clothes, plus a **skills marketplace** pitch: train at home, share skills elsewhere.

## How this sits next to L2 coverage

**RuntimeWire** (July 28, 2026) reported the L3 unveil and free **L2 → L3 upgrades** for existing preorder buyers, quoting the company’s “Human dexterity just got a lot cheaper” line. At that moment the site still highlighted L2 at **$1,288** / L2 Grande at **$1,322**; the current L3 page is the clearer **$1,688** commercial SKU. RuntimeWire also notes founder **Antonio Sitong Li**, YC **S26**, a four-person company manufacturing in San Francisco, and the earlier Columbia **Nori Bot** arXiv prototype at **$947** in parts.

Our prior Mechafeed piece covered Nori’s **sub-$2k / FCC-era** lab pitch. This post is the **L3 product-and-price** update from the live site, not a rehash of that regulatory angle.

## A Human's Take

A wheeled dual-arm box at appliance money is the shape of offer that actually gets into apartments. I’m watching three receipts: ships on the Fall 2026 window, whether 1.5 kg/arm holds up after a week of kitchen use, and how open the skills marketplace stays once preorders convert. Price is the hook; reliability is the product.
