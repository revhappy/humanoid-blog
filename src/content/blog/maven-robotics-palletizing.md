Maven Robotics came out of stealth with a machine that does not walk. A **wheeled base**, two arms, **vacuum suckers**. TechCrunch’s **September 10, 2026** exclusive says it rolls at **10 miles an hour** and lifts **30 kilograms**. The job is mixed palletizing: factory pallets in, store-specific mixed pallets out.

CEO **Hamza Derbas** told TechCrunch the company has **as many as eight robots** running **16 hours a day** with **99% or higher uptime**, after two years with a first customer and a few other partners.

## The work cell, not the pitch deck

Mixed palletizing is the unglamorous bottleneck. Identical goods arrive from plants. A warehouse has **48 hours** to rebuild pallets for what a store actually needs. Derbas: “It’s all done with human labor today, running around the warehouse picking one of this, one of that.”

At Maven’s Santa Clara training area, TechCrunch watched the robot pick and stack boxes with vacuum grippers. A live screen showed two units in a customer facility with people walking around them. The stack hooks a **warehouse management system** on one side and trucks on the other.

The GlobeNewswire release the same day names a **Fortune 250** consumer packaged goods customer running fleets over multiple shifts. Maven says it expects **more than 100,000 hours** of autonomous real-world operations by year-end 2026, and **1,000,000 hours** by the end of **2027**. Those are company targets, not audited hours.

## Why no legs

Derbas spent nine years in Apple’s special projects group, widely reported as the self-driving effort that wound down in **2024**. He started Maven that year with his brother **Khalid**, the CFO. The data loop is the AV one: pull logs in minutes or hours, retrain, ablate, redeploy.

On bipeds, he was not polite. Agility’s Digit is the closest industrial comparison. Derbas told TechCrunch legs “make zero sense for anything they’re doing…they are very complex, unreliable, and add unnecessary cost. ROI is the name of the game here.”

The round is **$100 million** Series A from **RoboStrategy**, **LocalGlobe**, **Vine Ventures**, and **XTX Ventures**, among others. The money is for **250** third-generation robots and early work on a fourth. Mixed-case palletizing and tote handling are an estimated **$80 billion** addressable market in Maven’s own numbers; the next step is messier material handling and assembly.

They built pincer-like gloves so humans can demo in the gripper’s form factor. Derbas: “We’re not in the race for models — we’re in the race to solve industrial labor.”

## A Human's Take

Wheels, vacuum, a WMS hook, and a claimed 16-hour shift. That is a more honest first product than another biped folding a shirt under lights. I will believe the 99% uptime when a named site publishes cycle time, faults per hour, and who still has to restack the ugly SKUs. Until then, this is the right shape of bet: one ugly workflow, run it until it pays.
