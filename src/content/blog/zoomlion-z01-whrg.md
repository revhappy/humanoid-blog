**Zoomlion** put its own humanoids on a Beijing track. A 16 September press release says the construction-equipment maker fielded independently developed robots at the second **World Humanoid Robot Games** in standing high jump, the 100-meter sprint, and standing long jump.

The company does not publish times or distances in that release. It frames the events as a stress test for gait code, joint response, and stability at speed, then points back to the factory.

## What the factory robots actually do

Zoomlion says it has deployed **dozens** of humanoids across its smart factories. Listed jobs:

- material sorting
- cable-harness tying
- component preassembly
- precision assembly
- finished-product inspection
- factory patrols

Two named bodies split the work. The bipedal **Z01** uses imitation learning to organize objects that were set down at random and to arrange and tie cable harnesses. The wheeled **Z03** is assigned precision processes, including preassembling mirrors for industrial equipment.

Those deployments sit on a larger plant: more than **300** intelligent production lines and more than **2,000** industrial robots running year-round, according to the same release. Zoomlion says operating data from the humanoids and from that installed base feeds back into models and hardware.

**Zeng Guang**, general manager of Zoomlion’s **ZValley**, said the Games measured more than speed and strength and gave the company a chance to benchmark against other teams.

The portfolio, as Zoomlion describes it, includes bipedal and wheeled humanoids plus quadrupeds, with in-house work on core components, operating systems, complete robots, and industrial applications. The release calls the company an “AI-native” robotics shop inside a high-end equipment maker. It does not give unit prices, takt times, or a named customer outside Zoomlion’s own plants.

## A Human's Take

A track meet is a demo with a stopwatch nearby. The sentence I care about is the harness-tying Z01 and the mirror-preassembling Z03 inside Zoomlion’s own factories. “Dozens” is still a small fleet next to 2,000 conventional industrial arms. If those humanoids are actually clearing a shift of randomly piled objects, that is the story. The sprint is advertising.
