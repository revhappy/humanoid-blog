Not every humanoid is chasing tote rates. **DroidUp** (also styled DroidUP / Zhuoyide in coverage), a Shanghai firm known for biped race platforms, has been showing **Moya** — a biomimetic humanoid aimed at **social presence**: warm skin, micro-expressions, and a compact Walker-family body under the costume.

## What coverage documents

**Futurism** (Feb 2026) and **Humanoid Press** database entries describe Moya as:

- Roughly **1.65 m** tall and about **32 kg** (database figures).
- **Silicone skin** held at approximately **human surface temperature** — Futurism cites about **89–96°F**; Humanoid Press lists **32–36°C**.
- A face capable of **joy/anger/sorrow/happiness**-style expressions, with **pupil tracking** that can meet a reporter’s gaze (Shanghai Media Group video cited by Futurism).
- Under the shell: **Walker 3** platform with cameras and lidar for path planning and obstacle avoidance, described as more compact than the marathon-tested Walker 2 lineage.

Founder **Li Qingdu**, quoted via SMB in Futurism’s piece, frames the product against “hard shells” that feel cold — arguing a robot that serves human life should be warm enough for people to connect with.

## Claims to handle carefully

DroidUp claims roughly **92% human-like walking accuracy**; Futurism notes the on-camera gait still shows audible actuators and a stiff walk, so treat the percentage as company messaging, not third-party measurement. Humanoid Press lists an estimated **~$173,000** price and **late 2026** sales targeting healthcare, education, and commercial interaction — again, database/secondary estimates, not a confirmed public order form in our sources.

## A Human's Take

I'm split: the thermal skin and gaze work are clever product design for care and hospitality, and also exactly the features that push uncanny-valley debates. Capability-wise I care whether Walker 3 under the costume can do a full shift of hallway navigation without a minder. Warm silicone sells the photo; runtime and recovery from falls sell the deployment.
