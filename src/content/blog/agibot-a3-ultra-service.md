**AGIBOT** posted on 17 September that its full-size **A3 Ultra** has left the factory pitch and is “already working in hotels, auto dealerships, supermarkets and metro inspection scenarios, with more deployments underway.” *Humanoids Daily* treated that X update as a company claim: the post does not give a fleet size or task-success numbers.

A same-day reprint of *Shanghai Observer* (*Shangguan*) is more specific. It says Zhiyuan (AGIBOT) released a commercial case video on **16 September** covering 4S auto shops, hotels, and convenience stores, and that the company told the paper those scenes are already live.

## What the video is supposed to show

*Shangguan*, via NetEase, lists the 4S-shop cut as multi-robot greeting, water service, model explanation, vehicle handover, flowers, photos, a ceremonial cannon, daily reports, and livestream selling. Hotel clips cover bed-making, room tidy, towel folding, check-in, elevator guiding, and night patrol. Convenience-store clips cover restocking, patrol, replenishment, heavy carrying, and customer help.

Those are AGIBOT’s own scene list. *Humanoids Daily* notes the product page is careful in a different way: A3 platform capability videos are **not** default A3 Ultra applications. Matching them takes extra programming, model work, site setup, and validation.

## The machine on the page

The store listing puts A3 Ultra at **174 cm** and **60 kg**, with **700 TOPS** onboard compute, **360°** LiDAR plus surround cameras, **20-DoF** tactile OmniHands, and a claimed **8-hour** battery when walking and standing time are equal. It advertises autonomous plug-in charging and a **10-second** battery swap. “24/7” on that page is the combination of those two, not a single charge.

Hands are rated from thin paper up to **5 kg**. Positioning is listed as UWB indoors and RTK outdoors, with accuracy under ±1 cm. The FAQ says A3 Ultra already holds EU CE (RED, safety, RoHS), US FCC, Canada IC, Japan MIC, and Australia RCM, plus UN38.3 and MSDS for shipping.

## A Human's Take

I like that the company is talking hotels and metro inspection instead of another kung-fu reel. I also want a count. “Already working” without hours, sites, or how often a person still has to step in is a trailer, not a logbook. The product page’s own footnote is the useful line: the cool A3 clips are not a default Ultra skill pack. If those 4S and hotel cuts are real customer floors, show the shift.
