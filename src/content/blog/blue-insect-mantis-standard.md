**Blue Insect Embodied**, a Xi’an Jiaotong University technology-transfer company, launched **Mantis Standard** (“Xiaobai”) on September 14. The starting price is **9,800 yuan**. TechNode and IT Home both carry that figure. 36Kr’s English edition translates the company as BlueBot Embodied Intelligence; the Chinese name is 蓝虫具身.

This is a wheeled upper-body platform, not a biped. Chassis, arms, waist, and head are separate modules joined with quick-install mechanical and electrical interfaces. 36Kr’s pitch is blunt: buy the arm for a desktop experiment, add the base when you need it to roll.

## The spec sheet

IT Home and TechNode list the same hardware numbers:

- **Height**: 1.4 m
- **Degrees of freedom**: 22 whole-body; 36Kr adds **7 DoF per arm**
- **Single-arm payload**: 7 kg
- **Arm span**: 663 mm
- **Teleop latency**: under 10 ms
- **Waist lift**: 550 mm, lift speed 200 mm/s
- **Base**: 3-DoF omnidirectional, 3 m/s forward
- **Runtime**: 3 hours

36Kr also lists **4K 60 fps** binocular vision, optional wrist cameras, and a 3-axis gimbal head. Control sits on the company’s **BW-Brain** running a “Wormhole” model family, with a “Quantum” VR teleop stack for teaching and data capture.

The company says the robot plugs into mainstream embodied-development stacks and ships with tools, tutorials, and project support. 36Kr notes an earlier **Mantis Pro** took bronze in mixed-material sorting at the World Humanoid Robot Games in August 2025. Standard is the cheaper, modular follow-on aimed at labs and light application work.

## A Human's Take

Nine thousand eight hundred yuan for a dual-arm mobile manipulator is the number that made me stop. It is a research cart with a lift column, not a factory biped, and that is fine. If the quick-swap interfaces actually let a lab keep the same arm when they add a base, this is the PC-builder model the humanoid aisle has been missing. I want a teardown of that 7 kg wrist before I trust the price.
