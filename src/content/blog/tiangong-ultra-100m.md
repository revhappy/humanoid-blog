**Tiangong Ultra**, built by the Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Center (X-Humanoid), ran 100 meters in **9.39 seconds** in a preliminary heat at the World Humanoid Robot Games on Saturday. That is faster than Usain Bolt’s 9.58-second human world record from 2009.

Honor’s **Lightning** finished the same heat in **9.47 seconds**, also under Bolt’s mark. SCMP, citing Reuters, says a Unitree robot came last in that heat at **12.41 seconds**. CGTN posted the full race from gun to tape.

## The year-over-year cut

Last year’s inaugural games, Tiangong Ultra won the 100 meters in **21.50 seconds**. SCMP and Reuters both flag that drop of more than 12 seconds as the useful comparison, not a like-for-like athletics record. Start procedures, timing gear, and how much autonomy sat in the loop are not fully spelled out in the coverage I fetched.

Honor said Lightning had already run **9.32 seconds** in a preparatory test, with a peak of **14.5 m/s**. The Guardian reports the same test figure and notes Lightning’s legs were lengthened by **10 cm** after the April half marathon, when it stood **169 cm** with **95 cm** legs.

Reuters, covering the Games on Sunday, says another humanoid of the same model as the 100-meter champion ran **400 meters in 39.7 seconds**, faster than Wayde van Niekerk’s 43.03-second human record.

AP reports a standing high jump of **2.88 meters** by an X-Humanoid machine, above last year’s humanoid best of **0.95 meters** and above Javier Sotomayor’s 2.45-meter human record.

## The rest of the meet

The Games run through August 26 at Beijing’s National Speed Skating Oval. Reuters lists **51 events**: **30** sports, **21** scenario contests. Huawei, a tech partner, says more than **40%** require full autonomy.

The less flashy tests are cable connection, EV charging, restaurant work, warehouse loading, and industrial assembly. Lumos Robotics CEO Yu Chao told Reuters running and jumping are not the point:

> “Only when it can work in those end scenarios does it have real value. Simply running and jumping does not improve efficiency.”

## A Human's Take

Nine-point-three-nine is a locomotion number, not a factory number. The clip that will stick with me is the foam and the hoist. If you cannot stop, you do not get to work next to people. I care more about whether the same platform can seat a cable on the first try than whether it beats Bolt in a heat with a crash mat.
