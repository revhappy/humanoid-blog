Collaborative-arm maker **DOBOT** has stepped further into consumer-facing bipeds. On **August 6, 2026**, 36Kr Europe reported the launch of **DOBOT LUMO**, described as a full-scenario “embodied all-terrain” humanoid aimed at homes, campuses, and outdoor recreation rather than factory totes.

## What the launch claims

According to the 36Kr write-up, LUMO is **nearly 1.3 meters** tall and is shown walking on **lawn, sand, and cobblestone**, switching between outdoor and indoor scenes. The piece frames the product as a multi-role companion: sports playmate outdoors, emotional companion at home, education partner for school-age kids, and stage performer for commercial events.

Brand marketing director **Xie Kaixuan** is quoted defining “embodied all-terrain” across four axes: multi-space mobility (home, mall, campus), multi-role identity, multimodal vision/voice/motion capability, and long-term learning with the user. The stack is said to run on DOBOT’s self-developed **Kongyi Embodied Large Model**, with claims of multimodal emotion perception, 3D spatial understanding with navigation and obstacle avoidance, and closed-loop learning from real-scene data.

## Context from the same coverage

The article positions LUMO for urban office workers, young families, and K–12 / research users who want an embodied teaching platform with secondary-development hooks (motion control, SLAM, vision, HRI). It does **not** publish a public U.S. MSRP or shipping calendar in the English 36Kr piece — those numbers stay unstated in our sources, so we leave them out.

DOBOT already sells industrial-leaning humanoids such as **Atom** (listed elsewhere with China/US store pricing). LUMO is a different product pitch: shorter stature, companion aesthetics, and outdoor gait demos rather than assembly-line dexterity.

## A Human's Take

I care less about “world’s first” marketing and more about what the video actually shows: continuous walking on real outdoor surfaces and a clean cut between sport and home modes. Emotion-recognition claims and backflip clips are easy to over-index. What I want next is runtime numbers, fall recovery, and whether LUMO can do a multi-hour campus shift without a minder. Until DOBOT publishes those, treat LUMO as a capable demo platform with a clear consumer target — not a proven home deployment.
