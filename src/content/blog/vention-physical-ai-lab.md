**Vention** opened a Physical AI lab in Montreal today. The work is robot arms on production lines, not humanoids. The company says the point is to take manipulation research and run it against the reliability, cost, and mess of a real factory.

**Dr. Jimmy Li**, Vention’s director of Physical AI and a McGill robotics researcher, runs it. **Dr. Joelle Pineau**, Cohere’s chief AI officer, joins as external technical advisor. The lab is **eight** people now, with more than **16** roles posted in control, simulation, and Physical AI.

## What they will train on

Vention’s PR puts the footprint at **90** of the Fortune 500, more than **28,000** machines, and a community of more than **6,000** factories. Physical AI is the fastest-growing segment, with related revenue up **400%** over the past year. BetaKit, citing CEO **Etienne Lacroix**, says the lab holds **16** of about **120** R&D staff and that the company raised a **$110 million** Series D in January.

The research list is industrial and unglamorous: data collection, motion planning, classical vision, vision foundation models, learning from demonstration, reinforcement learning. Target work is unstructured manipulation in industrial goods, electronics, and automotive, including a large unnamed automotive OEM on final-assembly tasks.

The lab already shipped **GRIIP**, launched in **February 2026**: scene digitalization, object segmentation, pose estimation, grasp selection, collision-free motion planning, mixing NVIDIA foundation models with Vention’s own. A public GRIIP SDK is promised so plant engineers can fork the pipeline.

> “The real opportunity is making these capabilities reliable, economical, and deployable across thousands of factories,” Lacroix said in the release.

> “Our clients aren’t waiting for a finished product to test; they’re in the room while we build it,” Li said.
