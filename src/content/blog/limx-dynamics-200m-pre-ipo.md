**LimX Dynamics** is treating a public listing like a survival skill. The Shenzhen humanoid startup said it closed a **US$200 million pre-IPO** round that values the company at **15 billion yuan** — about **$2.21 billion** on CNBC’s math, **$2.2 billion** in Nikkei’s write-up — and is already in a **confidential review** phase for an IPO, **likely in Hong Kong**.

Founder **Will Zhang** told reporters, per CNBC: “Listing is a must,” comparing the moment to Chinese EV startups that went public once the tech was “mature enough” — or risked fading.

## Who Wrote the Check

According to CNBC’s report on the company’s release, overseas participants included **Stone Venture** (UAE), **GGG** (Italy), and **Redstone VC** (Germany). Domestic and strategic names listed: **Lens Technology**, **IDG Capital**, **WestSummit Capital**, **Nio Capital**, and **Hefei Binhu Industry Development Group**.

The wider context is a funding spike. CNBC cited industry data provider Xiniu: China humanoid-sector investment hit **47.09 billion yuan (~$6.95B)** in Q2 — more than double Q1 and over six times the year-ago quarter. Unitree’s Shanghai fast-track and a thick Hong Kong queue sit in the same story.

## What LimX Is Selling

Company product pages describe **Oli** as a **165 cm**, **31-DoF** full-size general-purpose humanoid with modular end-effectors, quick-swap batteries, depth cameras, and dual compute for motion and perception. CNBC says LimX is aiming at fully autonomous commercial service robots, a multi-year plan to ship **thousands of humanoids to the Middle East**, and deliveries of entertainment-focused **Luna** units to South Korea customers.

Zhang’s strategic line, via CNBC: the “0 to 1” invention phase is done; the next moat is a product that meets user needs — and capital markets that can fund that grind.
