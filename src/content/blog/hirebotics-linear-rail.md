**Hirebotics** spent IMTS 2026 in Chicago showing two ways to stop forcing the part into a cobot’s envelope. **Line tracking** lets the **Cobot Painter** follow a conveyor. A **modular linear rail**, in **5-foot** increments or custom lengths, lets a welder, cutter, or painter roll along a long workpiece.

[Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/imts-2026-hirebotics-expands-cobot-reach-and-flexibility-with-new-line-tracking-and-linear-rail-capabilities) dated the floor report **16 September 2026**. *The Robot Report* ran the same capabilities on **19 September**. The company was in booth **236341** at McCormick Place, **14–19 September**, per its [IMTS page](https://www.hirebotics.com/newsroom/hirebotics-at-imts-2026/).

## Paint the part while the conveyor keeps moving

Hirebotics’ argument is simple: if parts already ride a finishing line, stopping them for a stationary cobot is extra handling. Line tracking uses an encoder and a sensor so the Cobot Painter keeps its taught path as conveyor speed changes. A sensor sees the arriving part and starts the job.

Setup stays inside **Beacon**, the same no-code, cloud app the company uses to teach welding, cutting, and painting. **Matthew Bush**, co-founder and CEO, told Robotics 24/7 that shops should not have to redesign the line around the arm.

The Cobot Painter itself is a **FANUC CRX-10iA/L Paint** on Beacon. Hirebotics’ site lists the painter at **$140,000** as a complete system, the welder from **$105,000**, and the cutter from **$100,000**.

## A rail instead of flipping a 12-foot frame

A Hirebotics cobot reach is quoted at **68.9 inches**. Robotics 24/7 says the fixed work envelope is nearly **140 inches**. That still loses to a 12-foot frame or a 20-foot weldment.

The new rail is meant to sit under the same family of systems. It is **available now with manual positioning**. Beacon position verification is planned. A fully automated axis is planned for **the end of 2026**. On the Hirebotics product pages, Linear Rail still reads “coming soon” as an add-on line item; the show coverage is the source for availability of the manual version.

Bush’s other point: customers already invent mounts (magnetic Magswitch, inverted, headstock-tailstock). The rail is another option, not a new programming language.

## A Human's Take

This is the unglamorous version of “make the robot bigger.” You do not need a 20-foot arm if you can slide a 69-inch arm down a beam. Manual rail plus a promised auto axis in late 2026 is the honest product state. I care whether Beacon actually knows where the carriage is before anyone lets it weld on a moving fixture. Until that verification ships, treat the rail as a better table, not a seventh axis.
